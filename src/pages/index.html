Mira Nencheva

I am a postdoctoral fellow in psychology at Stanford University, working with Michael Frank in the Language and Cognition Lab and Ian Gotlib in the Stanford Neurodevelopment, Affect, and Psychopathology Lab.

My research asks how moment-to-moment shifts in how young children and their caregivers feel shape, and are shaped by the way they communicate and learn. I believe that to truly understand our remarkable human capacity to navigate communication in a complex emotional world, we need to not only look to the early development of the systems that support it, but also take an ecological approach that captures the real-world contexts in which these systems develop.

I study the dynamics of affect and communication in infancy and early childhood through a multi-pronged approach. I leverage computational tools to detect patterns in children's everyday environments, and I use neural, physiological, and behavioral measures to capture how children process these dynamic cues in real time.

Before Stanford, I completed my Ph.D. in psychology at Princeton University with Casey Lew-Williams and Diana Tamir, and earned an M.A. at Dartmouth College and a B.S. in Symbolic Systems at Stanford.
